NVIDIA DGX Spark

NVIDIA DGX Spark is a compact desktop AI computer built around the GB10 Grace Blackwell chip, for running and developing local models.

Last updated 31 Oct 2026.
What changed: Dell Pro Precision with GB10 price updated.

Best for fitting large models in 128 GB Best for CUDA development and fine-tuning Best for small teams and two-unit clusters

Labels drawn from what Tom's Hardware, The Register, ServeTheHome, Level1Techs, Alex Ziskind, StorageReview and NetworkChuck say it suits.

The short version
  • Praised: 128 GB of unified memory and NVIDIA's CUDA software. It is quiet and compact.
  • Criticized: the price and the memory bandwidth.
  • Speed: The Register found generation about as fast as a cheaper Strix Halo mini PC. It is slower than high-end GPUs.
  • Suits: developers and small teams who want capacity and CUDA more than raw generation speed.

Pros extracted from reviews, with who said it.

Flaws but not dealbreakers

Cons extracted from reviews, with who said it.

  • –Price is the most-cited drawback Mentioned by Tom's Hardware, The Register (both articles: first look, comparison), HotHardware, NetworkChuck, Digital Spaceport
  • –273 GB/s memory bandwidth limits token generation Mentioned by ServeTheHome, LMSYS, The Register, Digital Spaceport
  • –Generation speed about the same as a Strix Halo mini PC at two-thirds to half the price Mentioned by The Register (Strix Halo comparison)
  • –Slower at generation than discrete GPUs and multi-GPU rigs Mentioned by LMSYS (RTX 5090 and RTX Pro 6000), NetworkChuck (dual RTX 4090), Digital Spaceport (quad RTX 3090)
  • –Much CUDA software assumes x86, which adds setup work on the Spark's ARM64 Mentioned by Simon Willison
  • –Network throughput below the advertised 200 Gb/s in tests Mentioned by Level1Techs, ServeTheHome (about 96 Gbps in iperf3)
  • –Some would trade the fast network ports for a lower price Mentioned by The Register (first look)
  • –Not a general-purpose PC: runs NVIDIA's Linux software, no Windows Mentioned by Tom's Hardware, The Register, Tim Carambat
  • –Warm, audible fans under sustained heavy load Mentioned by NetworkChuck

Noise, power and heat

Measured or heard by the sources, with the source named.

  • Noise: Under 40 dBA outside stress tests (ServeTheHome); a peak of 37.5 dBA at 18 inches under extended image generation, noise floor 33 dBA (Tom's Hardware).
  • Power: 60–90 W in LLM inference and just under 200 W with CPU and GPU loaded (ServeTheHome).
  • Heat: "Runs cool and quiet" (HotHardware).

Who it is for

What reviewers say about the buyer.

  • Suits: Buyers who want 128 GB of unified memory and the CUDA stack for local AI. Tom's Hardware: "you need to be all in on AI to justify one".
  • Less suited: Buyers who mainly want fast token generation: 273 GB/s of memory bandwidth limits it (ServeTheHome, LMSYS).

Where reviews disagree

Differences between sources, side by side.

  • Noise: ServeTheHome, HotHardware and StorageReview call it quiet; Tom's Hardware says it "isn't silent under load".
  • Software: Tom's Hardware credits the proven CUDA ecosystem; Simon Willison found CUDA on ARM64 hard to navigate at first and says NVIDIA's documentation improved fast.
  • Value: The Register found generation speed about the same as a Strix Halo mini PC at two-thirds to half the price.

Tom's Hardware
Review siteReview: "…a fast and fun AI toolbox that beats out AMD's Ryzen AI Max+ 395"

Nvidia's Grace Blackwell mini-PC is a winner, but you need to be all in on AI to justify one.

Praises the efficient GB10, 128 GB of RAM, the CUDA ecosystem and clustering; calls it pricey if you won't use its features.

Positive
ServeTheHome
Review site⚑ NVIDIA-supplied pre-production unitReview: "The GB10 Machine is so Freaking Cool"

It is, however, impossible for me to get past the feeling that this is a game-changer.

Likes the 128 GB memory, 200GbE networking and a quiet chassis, while saying it is not "the most mature NVIDIA platform" in its pre-release state.

Positive
The Register (first look)
Review siteFirst look: "Nvidia's tiniest supercomputer, tackles large models at solid speeds"

…a system that may not be the fastest at any one thing, but can run them all.

Capacity and CUDA compatibility are strengths; bandwidth limits generation speed, and many buyers "would have happily forgone the high-speed networking in favor of a lower MSRP".

Mixed
The Register (Strix Halo comparison)
Review siteComparison: "Tested: AMD's Strix Halo vs Nvidia's DGX Spark"

For between two-thirds and half the price, you could get a Strix Halo box that churns out tokens just as quickly.

The Spark's GPU is roughly 2–3× faster to first token and better for image and video work, for those who want an AI appliance for prototyping agents, fine-tuning models or generating text, image and video, it is "probably the better choice, assuming you can stomach the asking price".

Mixed
HotHardware

At 4 grand, the DGX Spark is not an enthusiast's toy, but rather a real AI machine for serious business.

Finds it quiet and cool under load and gives it its "HotHardware Recommended" award; its Mac Studio comparison is, by its own account, not a perfect one.

Positive
